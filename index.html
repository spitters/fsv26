Course material

During this course we use the Rocq proof assistant (formerly Coq). Rocq is both a functional programming language and a system for reasoning about one's programs using formal proofs.

Installation. The easiest route is Docker and VS Code (Rocq 9.0, VsRocq, QuickChick, Equations preinstalled; the same image is used by the autograder). Alternatively, install the Rocq Platform 2025.08.3 (Rocq 9.0, the version the course material is tested with; see install) and choose an IDE; VS Code with VsRocq is recommended.

In the browser. The lectures use executable slides: every code block runs in the browser (no installation). The full books are available the same way. The Rocq panel opens on the right; the first visit downloads about 40 MB of libraries, so wait for the package list to finish before stepping. If the panel is missing, click the plug icon at the right edge or add ?jscoq=on to the URL. Use a recent desktop Chrome or Firefox. Scratchpad for live experiments. Imp in WebAssembly runs the evaluator of the ImpCEvalFun chapter as compiled code, extracted from Rocq rather than interpreted by it. The QuickChick volume is read-only in the browser; run QuickChick in Docker.

We use part of the online textbook Software Foundations by Benjamin C. Pierce et al. (Logical Foundations v7.0, Programming Language Foundations, QuickChick). Links below go to the copies hosted here; the upstream pages are equivalent.

Homework

The course includes a track of weekly exercises, handed in through Classroom 50. Submission and approval of 10 mandatory assignments is required: five assignments from the first seven weeks and five from the second. Work in groups of two (exceptionally, three) is encouraged; one hand-in per group. The exercises are handed in each Friday before 11:00. To hand in, accept each assignment through its link in the schedule below (one repository per group; the first group member accepts and invites the others as collaborators); your hand-in is the last commit on the default branch before the deadline. Come prepared to the Q&A sessions so you can ask questions in person.

Rocq must accept the homework handed in as a .v file in its entirety. The exercise will not be graded if Rocq does not accept the file. Use Admitted to make Rocq accept incomplete proofs.

You are not allowed to use Rocq tactics in the homework that we have not yet discussed (for example tauto, eauto and lia are forbidden).

Rocq project

The course finishes with a Rocq project; the list of suggestions is here, with further topics on data structures, language features and security. You may also invent a Rocq project yourself, or do a variation or extension of one of the suggested projects. If you invent a project yourself or use a variation of the suggestions, you have to discuss it with the lecturer.

You are allowed to work in groups, and a group writes one report together and submits the same Rocq file. A bigger effort is expected from a group, which usually means completing the very difficult extensions. Each of you remains responsible for all of the code and the report, and the oral exam tests your understanding of all the material.

Teamwork means pair programming: both of you at the same development, taking turns at the keyboard and discussing every step. It does not mean one of you doing most of the work, nor dividing the exercises between you. Help is allowed — a classmate or an AI assistant explaining an error message, suggesting which lemma might apply, or pointing you at the right chapter. Handing in work you cannot account for is not: a project resting on a team mate, on an AI assistant, or on a solution found online is cheating. Your knowledge of the project will be tested in the exam. Acknowledge everyone and everything that helped you, AI assistants included, and in what capacity.

To complete the Rocq project, you deliver:

  • Registration of your final project on Brightspace, due Thursday 22 October 2026 at 12:00. Discuss your choice with the lecturer before then, especially when proposing a project of your own design or choosing one of the experimental further topics.
  • A set of Rocq .v files containing the solution to your project and a written report, submitted on Brightspace, due Monday 14 December 2026 at 9:00.

The report follows the requirements in the projects document: ten pages is an upper bound and shorter is fine; it gives an introduction, the main results, conclusions with your acknowledgements, and a bibliography if you cite literature. Name the authors and their student numbers at the top, describe the project in your own words rather than copying its description, and say which problems you ran into and how you solved them.

Do not include unnecessarily long excerpts of Rocq code; use small fragments to illustrate a point. Informal proofs are formulated as taught during the course. Do not paraphrase Rocq proofs in natural language ("now we apply tactic X so our goal becomes Y").

Grading

The grade reflects an overall assessment of:

  • the group project;
  • an individual oral examination, which also tests your understanding of all the material in the course.

Only students with approved exercises can attend the oral exam and thus pass the course.

The grade of the Rocq project is based on:

  1. Correctness: whether the Rocq definitions and lemmas correctly model the problem.
  2. Completeness: whether all lemmas are proven (no lemmas omitted, no proofs finished with admit or Admitted).
  3. Style: whether the Rocq code follows the style guidelines (bullets, sensible variable names, use of implicit arguments).
  4. Effectiveness: whether the definitions and proofs are given in an effective way (no useless proof steps; the induction tactic not used blindly).
  5. Report: style, presentation, language and correctness of the report.

During the oral exam we test your understanding of the project, e.g. by asking you to explain proofs of the project informally, by querying you about the contents of your report, or by asking general questions about the contents of the course.
